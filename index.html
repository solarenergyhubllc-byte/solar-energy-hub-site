Columbia, Missouri | Phase A in planning

Solar trees, shade and switchgrass on a Missouri research farm.

Solar Energy Hub, LLC is planning a solar and agrivoltaic research campus on a university research farm near an existing broadcast tower: solar trees that could help power the tower, shade for cattle and other animals, battery backup and switchgrass fuels research.

What we plan to build

Original concept drawing, not to scale. It shows the idea only. Nothing is designed, surveyed or filed.

  1. 1

    Solar trees

    Solar trees of 40 to 50 kW each, standing in pasture. Each one is a pole with a panel canopy: it makes power and casts shade. How many we build depends on the tower's load, which we have not measured, and on the research design.

  2. 2

    Shade and research

    Cattle and other animals stand in the shade, so researchers can study heat stress. Switchgrass for biogas and ethanol research, plus farm robots and AI implements, share the same acres.

  3. 3

    Battery storage

    Storage for off-grid backup of critical loads. It is sized after a load study.

  4. 4

    Power link to the tower

    A proposed private line to help offset the power the tower uses. There is no agreement with the tower's owner, no load study and nothing filed.

  5. 5

    Grid tie-in

    A proposed connection to the existing overhead lines. We understand they belong to the local electric utility. No application has been filed.

One tree, many jobs

The solar tree is the building block. Each one is meant to do several jobs at once on the same acre: make power, shade animals, watch the weather, serve as a base for farm drones and carry a small research module. Everything below is a concept to be tested with the University and engineers. Nothing is designed or built.

Original concept drawing. On a phone, slide the picture sideways. The four boxes at the bottom show how the same tree works through a day.

Power

40 to 50 kW of solar per tree: a canopy of limbs plus flexible solar wrapped on the pole from 10 ft up. Power runs inside the pole and underground to storage.

Shade and research

The canopy shades cattle and other animals so researchers can measure heat stress, while switchgrass and other crops grow nearby.

Weather and data

Sensors on each tree for wind, temperature and rain. Data sharing with a TV station or the National Weather Service would need their agreement.

Drone base Concept

A small building on the tree holds a drone dock with a charging pad. Drones could inspect crops and animals, seed test plots and patrol the grounds. Flights follow FAA rules. Moving live animals by drone is not planned.

Connectivity and security

A radio and satellite link on each tree could give the farm and the tower a backup data path, and carry security cameras. Security would be built into hardware, such as hardware security modules, with quantum-safe encryption options to be evaluated. The US Department of Energy notes that smaller solar systems have no cybersecurity standards, so we plan to follow its guidance. We do not plan to jam anyone's signals: that is illegal.

CO2-capture module Research

A pilot sleeve on the pole to test capturing CO2 from air. Capture takes a lot of energy, so this is a small experiment and not a climate claim.

How many trees, how much land?

One 45 kW tree makes about 63 MWh a year at our planning rate of 1,400 kWh per kW. The table shows trees needed to match a tower's yearly energy use, and the land at 1 to 5 acres per tree. The tower's real load has not been measured, so these rows are examples, not findings.

Backup is sized by hours, not by gigawatts. Long-duration storage is rated in power (MW) and hours. For example, a 1 MW load carried for 100 hours needs 100 MWh. A 1 GW system would be a utility-scale plant. Solar trees cannot run a tower at night, so storage and the grid fill the gaps. The size will be set after the tower's load is measured and the University's land is confirmed.

Crop and materials research on the same land

Side-by-side plots of switchgrass, miscanthus, bamboo and winter wheat, each with and without tree shade, designed with University researchers. Bamboo is a long-lived plant, so it gets its own plots rather than a rotation, and only cold-hardy, non-running types would be considered. Switchgrass and bamboo fibre could feed fuel and new-materials research. Yields for Missouri are not yet proven.

The land

A university research farm, 250 to 300 acres, discussed verbally and not yet in writing. The solar trees are meant to stand over working pasture, so farming and research continue beneath them.

Power for the tower Proposal only

We would like to help offset the power the tower uses. We have not measured its load and have no agreement with its owner. Storage could keep a critical load running off-grid.

Wind, later

Wind is not in Phase A. It may return as a later option once on-site data exist. Measured tall-tower wind data for Columbia exist: Forecasting Wind Speeds at Tall Tower Heights Within Missouri, University of Missouri doctoral dissertation, 2022. Those instruments sit at 68 m and higher, above short-turbine heights.

How Phase A can be funded

Phase A uses no debt. We are re-costing the budget after a change of plan, and we will publish a figure once quotes arrive. Nothing has been raised yet, and every figure on this page is a plan, not a commitment.
