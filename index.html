The problem: farms are asked to choose

Land

Ground-mount solar uses about 5 to 7 acres per megawatt, often taken out of crops and grazing.

Heat

Heat stress costs US livestock producers about $2.5 billion a year (Ohio State), and Missouri's 1.86 million beef cows (USDA, Jan. 2025) graze mostly open pasture with little shade.

Energy

Farms want lower costs and new income, and critical rural sites still rely on diesel for backup power.

Most solutions solve one of these and cost the other two.

The solution: solar that shares the farm

Raised canopy rows

Long rows of solar raised high enough for farm equipment and livestock to pass underneath. Design target: about 450 kW per quarter-mile row; 11 rows make about 5 MW. Power feeds multi-day storage so a critical site can run without diesel, with surplus power to the grid.

Research solar tree

A tree-form structure of about 50 kW on a single support, giving moving shade for cattle and a platform for drone charging, farm sensors and carbon-capture research. Further technical details are shared with partners under a confidentiality agreement.

Who we serve first

Missouri first, then the Midwest cattle and crop belt.

  • 1
    Research and university farms: first pilot with measured data
  • 2
    Critical rural sites such as towers, clinics and water systems: solar plus multi-day backup instead of diesel
  • 3
    Mid-size Missouri cattle operations: shade plus on-farm power
  • 4
    Rural electric co-ops and utilities: local clean power, with land kept in farming

How the business makes money

First, power and backup-power contracts at critical rural sites. Then farm sales and leases through local installers from 2028, maintenance contracts, and design licensing to manufacturers.
